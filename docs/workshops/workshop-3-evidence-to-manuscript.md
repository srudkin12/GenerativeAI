# Workshop 3 — From evidence to manuscript

## Purpose

Help participants convert verified evidence into a manuscript while preventing AI-assisted revision from silently changing the authorised argument.

## Recommended duration

Approximately 2 hours.

## Participant preparation

Bring one of:

- a results section;
- an abstract;
- a discussion paragraph;
- a mature outline;
- a claim supported by a verified research object.

## Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:

- distinguish evidence from authorised claim;
- construct a claim-preservation contract;
- classify writing requirements by priority;
- use AI to revise within a bounded admissible space;
- link substantive paragraphs to evidence through an evidence ledger;
- recognise when a writing problem must be returned to analysis or interpretation.

## Session structure

### 0–20 min — Evidence is not the same as claim

Participants identify:

- verified research object;
- strongest authorised claim;
- tempting stronger claim;
- qualification that must remain.

### 20–45 min — Build the claim-preservation contract

Complete the [Claim-Preservation Contract](../../templates/claim-preservation-contract.md).

Focus on:

- authorised claims;
- prohibited claims;
- inferential boundaries;
- required qualifications;
- frozen distinctions.

### 45–60 min — Revision under ordered constraints

Introduce:

1. verified evidence and correctness;
2. claim-preservation contract;
3. compatible outlet/editor/referee requirements;
4. outlet/corpus overlay;
5. general writing heuristics;
6. local house style.

Participants classify proposed revisions by where they sit in the hierarchy.

### 60–75 min — Break

### 75–95 min — AI-assisted rewriting test

Provide the model with:

- a paragraph;
- the claim-preservation contract;
- the desired writing objective.

Ask it to revise the paragraph.

Then audit:

- Did claim strength change?
- Was a qualification dropped?
- Did a conceptual distinction collapse?
- Did journal-style language override the evidence boundary?

### 95–110 min — Evidence ledger

Complete one row of the [Evidence Ledger](../../templates/evidence-ledger.md) for each substantive paragraph under review.

### 110–120 min — Final sufficiency audit

Use the [Post-Revision Sufficiency Audit](../../templates/post-revision-sufficiency-audit.md).

Classify every failure:

- presentation repair;
- claim-authorisation reconsideration;
- analytical/evidential work.

## Take-away artefact

Participants leave with:

- a Claim-Preservation Contract;
- an Evidence Ledger;
- a Post-Revision Sufficiency Audit;
- a model-independent instruction for future AI-assisted manuscript revision.

## PISA teaching episodes

Use:

- PCA/Ball Mapper claim-preservation example;
- withdrawn B4→B9 interpretation;
- direct engagement with the finished scholarly object.

## Facilitator caution

Do not let "better writing" become the unexamined objective.

The workshop asks whether revision is **admissible**, not merely whether it is fluent.
