# Workshop 1 — Building an AI-assisted research workflow

## Purpose

Help participants design a research workflow in which generative AI can widen the proposal space without becoming the unexamined authority over the research object.

## Recommended duration

Approximately 2 hours.

## Participant preparation

Bring one of:

- a current research idea;
- an existing dataset or corpus;
- a policy/public-data question;
- an early-stage manuscript problem.

No coding is required.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:

- distinguish the substantive research problem from the AI task;
- specify the analytical object and non-negotiable constraints;
- identify productive AI roles at different research stages;
- distinguish candidate generation from verified evidence;
- decide which consequential decisions require documentation.

## Session structure

### 0–15 min — The problem before the prompt

Participants write a three-sentence research problem without mentioning AI.

Pair critique:

- Is there a real social-science problem?
- What knowledge does the researcher already bring?
- What would remain interesting if AI disappeared?

### 15–35 min — Widen the proposal space

Participants ask a generative system for:

- three alternative questions;
- one uncomfortable comparison;
- one alternative specification;
- one assumption the researcher may be overlooking.

Exercise rule:

**No proposal is accepted merely because it is novel or fluent.**

### 35–55 min — Specify the analytical object

Complete the [AI-Assisted Research Charter](../../templates/ai-assisted-research-charter.md).

Focus on:

- unit of analysis;
- inclusions/exclusions;
- aggregation;
- target of inference;
- required caveats.

### 55–70 min — Break

### 70–90 min — Generated artefact versus evidence

Participants classify example outputs:

- model-suggested reference;
- generated R/Python code;
- model-produced theme;
- locally executed estimate;
- inspected archival document;
- AI-written interpretation.

For each, ask:

**What would need to happen before this could support a research claim?**

### 90–110 min — First consequential decision

Complete an [AI-Assisted Research Decision Log](../../templates/ai-assisted-research-decision-log.md) for one decision made during the workshop.

### 110–120 min — Close

Each participant states:

1. the substantive problem;
2. one role AI may play;
3. one thing AI is not authorised to decide.

## Take-away artefact

Participants leave with:

- an AI-Assisted Research Charter;
- a first Decision Log;
- a list of project-specific verification boundaries.

## PISA teaching episodes

Use:

- moving-benchmark question;
- UK4 specification;
- generated package/local execution distinction.

## Facilitator caution

Do not reward the most elaborate prompt.

Reward the clearest research problem, strongest specification and most explicit verification boundary.
