# Workshop 2 — Challenging the research

## Purpose

Use generative AI as an adversarial research instrument rather than a system for making the preferred interpretation more persuasive.

## Recommended duration

Approximately 2 hours.

## Participant preparation

Bring:

- one result, interpretation, qualitative theme, model comparison or substantive claim that currently feels important;
- enough context to explain why the claim matters.

## Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:

- identify the inferential assumptions behind a preferred claim;
- formulate a named threat rather than generic "robustness";
- ask AI for disconfirming rather than supportive analysis;
- distinguish a result from the interpretation attached to it;
- record when a claim should be retained, narrowed, reframed or withdrawn.

## Session structure

### 0–20 min — State the claim you most want to keep

Participants complete:

> My current preferred claim is...

Then:

> The evidence would have to establish... for that claim to be warranted.

### 20–40 min — Generate the hostile question

Ask AI to behave as:

- a sceptical domain expert;
- a conventional-method reviewer;
- an identification critic;
- a measurement critic.

Participants choose the **single strongest** challenge.

### 40–60 min — Name the threat

Complete the first half of the [Claim Challenge Record](../../templates/claim-challenge-record.md).

A good robustness exercise must answer:

- What threat is being tested?
- What changes?
- What remains stable?
- What conclusion follows?

### 60–75 min — Break

### 75–95 min — Result versus inference

Use the PISA B4→B9 episode.

Exercise:

Given a numerical result, write:

1. what was observed/computed;
2. what additional assumption is required for the preferred interpretation;
3. what evidence would distinguish the two.

### 95–110 min — Revise the claim

Complete either:

- the remainder of the Claim Challenge Record; or
- an [Inference Revision Record](../../templates/inference-revision-record.md).

Final status must be one of:

- retained;
- narrowed;
- reframed;
- withdrawn;
- requires new analysis.

### 110–120 min — Provenance reflection

Ask:

**What evidence now supports the story about why the claim changed?**

Distinguish contemporaneous trace from retrospective explanation.

## Take-away artefact

Participants leave with:

- a completed Claim Challenge Record;
- an Inference Revision Record where needed;
- an explicit account of what evidence would falsify or narrow the preferred interpretation.

## PISA teaching episodes

Use:

- PCA narrowing the Ball Mapper claim;
- B4→B9 correction;
- landmark-order robustness if additional material is needed.

## Facilitator caution

The objective is not to make every claim smaller.

The objective is to make the final claim no stronger than the evidence licenses.
