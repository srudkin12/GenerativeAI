# Reason provenance in plain social-science language

Research transparency often asks **what happened**. Reason provenance asks a narrower additional question:

> **What evidence supports a claim about how a consequential research move entered the project, who or what contributed to it, and how it changed the research trajectory?**

The aim is not to reconstruct every mental state. The aim is to make **bounded claims about contribution** proportionate to the evidence available.

## Four useful contribution functions

### Origination

Who or what first proposed a consequential question, distinction, interpretation or analytical move within the traceable workflow?

Origination is not the same as ownership of the final research project.

### Evaluation

Who or what judged whether a proposal, result or interpretation was acceptable, weak, misleading or in need of further testing?

Evaluation may be visible through acceptance, rejection, requests for robustness or decisions to narrow a claim.

### Integration

Who or what connected a proposal or result to the wider design, literature, manuscript or sequence of research decisions?

A useful suggestion becomes part of scholarship only after it is integrated into a defensible research object.

### Interpretation

Who or what attached substantive meaning to the empirical result?

Interpretation is especially important because a numerical result can be correctly calculated while the inferential meaning attached to it is not supported.

## Attribution ambiguity

Hybrid workflows can make attribution difficult because the same final sentence or result may be compatible with several different histories of contribution.

More records do not automatically solve that problem. What matters is whether the available evidence can distinguish the histories relevant to the claim being made.

## Three evidence timings

A practical project may contain:

1. **Ex-ante governance** — rules or constraints specified before a move is made.
2. **Contemporaneous trace** — records produced while consequential decisions are being made.
3. **Ex-post reconstruction** — later explanations of why the process unfolded as it did.

Ex-post reflection can be valuable. It should not be silently treated as if it were contemporaneous evidence.

## A bounded approach

Reason provenance is not an unrestricted logging regime.

Document the decisions that would materially change:

- the analytical object;
- the inferential claim;
- the inclusion or exclusion of evidence;
- the interpretation of results;
- the contribution claimed by a method;
- the final scholarly conclusion;
- the attribution of intellectual contribution.

The templates in this repository are designed around those consequential points.

## Reason provenance is not manuscript governance

Reason provenance concerns evidence for claims about **contribution and trajectory**.

[Manuscript governance](manuscript-governance/index.md) concerns whether a proposed textual revision **preserves the authorised research claim**.

A revision may be perfectly attributable yet still substantively inadmissible. Conversely, a permissible revision may not require a detailed contribution record unless attribution itself matters.
