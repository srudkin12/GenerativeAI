# Ten-step protocol for Generative-AI-assisted social-science research


> **Do not use this protocol mechanically.** The workflow can itself create failure modes, including delegation creep, novelty erosion, salience substitution and performative oversight. Read [Risks & Failure Modes](risks-failure-modes.html) alongside the protocol.


This protocol is the practical takeaway from the seminar.

It is not a universal recipe and it does not replace disciplinary or methodological expertise. It is a compact governance structure for projects in which generative AI contributes to consequential research activity.

## 1. Start with a substantive research problem

Begin with a research problem, public event, theoretical puzzle, dataset, policy question or empirical anomaly that matters independently of generative AI.

Ask:

- What is substantively interesting?
- Why does the problem belong in this discipline?
- What prior knowledge or methodological programme do I bring?

**Do not begin with:** "What can I get AI to do?"

## 2. Use AI to widen the proposal space

Invite generative AI to propose alternative research questions, comparisons, analytical specifications, robustness checks, counter-arguments and relevant search directions.

Treat these as **candidate moves**, not evidence.

Record proposals only when they materially change the research trajectory.

## 3. Specify the analytical object and non-negotiable constraints

Make the object of analysis explicit.

Record constraints that should not be silently changed, such as the unit of analysis, inclusion/exclusion rules, aggregation decisions, estimand or inferential target, known data limitations and required disciplinary distinctions.

Human contribution often appears here as specification, constraint or refusal.

## 4. Separate generated artefacts from verified evidence

Generated code, prose, tables, interpretations or citations are research artefacts.

They become eligible to support a research claim only after the verification appropriate to the research design.

For quantitative work this may include local execution, diagnostics and reproducibility checks. For qualitative, archival or mixed-method work it may include source inspection, coding checks, analytic documentation, triangulation or other design-appropriate verification.

## 5. Challenge the preferred interpretation

Ask what evidence would make the preferred story weaker.

Use conventional comparators, robustness checks tied to named threats, adversarial questions, alternative operationalisations, sensitivity analysis, disconfirming cases or competing substantive explanations.

A useful challenge should be allowed to narrow the contribution.

## 6. Record claim changes

Track whether important claims are unchanged, revised, narrowed, reframed, withdrawn or new.

Keep the reason for the change and the evidence that triggered it.

A correctly calculated quantity may still support an unwarranted substantive inference.

## 7. Authorise the research claim before substantial rewriting

Once the evidence is sufficiently mature, state the current claim boundary explicitly.

A claim-preservation contract should identify authorised claims, prohibited claims, inferential boundaries, required qualifications and frozen conceptual or terminological distinctions.

This provides the interface between research and manuscript production.

## 8. Govern manuscript revision

Use manuscript governance to ensure that clearer, shorter or more journal-compatible prose does not silently strengthen or alter the research argument.

Evidence and claim preservation outrank outlet style, rhetorical force, fluency and local house preferences.

When a writing problem is actually an evidence or interpretation problem, return upstream.

## 9. Inspect the finished scholarly object directly

Read the final object as a reader will encounter it.

Check figures and visual semantics, claims and qualifications, references and DOI metadata, identity and affiliation metadata, document properties, and consistency across abstract, text, tables, figures and supplements.

Final inspection is a distinct research task.

## 10. Retain enough provenance to justify later contribution claims

Keep evidence proportionate to the claims you may later need to make about contribution.

Distinguish ex-ante rules, contemporaneous trace, frozen project evidence and ex-post reconstruction.

Do not assume that a persuasive retrospective story is equivalent to contemporaneous evidence.

---

# What the protocol is trying to achieve

The aim is not to maximise documentation or minimise AI use.

The aim is to make consequential research judgement **visible, challengeable and auditable** while retaining the speed and breadth that generative AI can bring to scholarly work.

A compact summary is:

**substantive problem → proposal generation → governed specification → verified evidence → adversarial challenge → claim revision → claim authorisation → manuscript governance → final-object inspection → bounded provenance**
