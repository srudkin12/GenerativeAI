# An integrated governance architecture

The resource distinguishes four logical layers in an AI-assisted research workflow.

## Layer A — Evidence production

This layer owns the activities that establish whether a research object is verified.

Depending on the project, that may include:

- data acquisition and cleaning;
- coding and measurement;
- statistical modelling;
- qualitative analysis;
- archival or documentary analysis;
- simulation or formal derivation;
- diagnostics and reproducibility checks.

Generated code or prose can participate in this layer, but generation alone does not verify a result.

**Authority:** evidence appropriate to the research design.

## Layer B — Substantive interpretation and claim authorisation

Verified evidence does not write its own interpretation.

Domain knowledge, theory, design and researcher judgement determine what the evidence licenses.

A **claim-preservation contract** can make that boundary explicit through:

- authorised claims;
- prohibited claims;
- inferential boundaries;
- required qualifications;
- frozen conceptual or terminological distinctions.

**Authority:** verified evidence plus substantive and inferential judgement.

## Layer C — Manuscript governance and drafting

The manuscript can now be reorganised, clarified, compressed, expanded or adapted to disciplinary and outlet conventions.

The Academic Writing Filter belongs here.

Revision is treated as an **admissibility problem**: presentation can change only within the space permitted by the evidence, claim-preservation contract and legitimate scholarly-context constraints.

**Authority:** higher-order research and claim constraints govern lower-order presentation choices.

## Layer D — Adversarial and release audit

A final audit checks whether the finished scholarly object still corresponds to the evidence and authorised argument.

A failure should be routed correctly:

- presentation problem → Layer C;
- claim-boundary problem → Layer B;
- evidence or analytical problem → Layer A.

A validity problem should not be repaired by rhetoric.

# Where reason provenance fits

Reason provenance is **cross-cutting** rather than a fifth production layer.

It asks what evidence permits bounded claims about who or what contributed to consequential moves within and across the layers.

For example:

- Who first proposed the consequential research question?
- Who imposed the analytical specification or refused an alternative?
- What evidence shows that a preferred claim was narrowed after challenge?
- Who introduced an interpretation that was later withdrawn?
- What record supports the claim that a qualification was deliberately preserved during revision?

Reason provenance therefore concerns the **evidential basis of contribution claims**.

Manuscript governance concerns the **admissibility of transformations of the scholarly representation**.

The concepts are related, but they solve different problems.
