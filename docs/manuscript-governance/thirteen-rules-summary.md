# Academic Writing Filter: thirteen operating rules in compact form

The October 2026 Academic Writing Filter contains thirteen default operating rules.

They are **not thirteen universal laws of good writing**. They operate beneath the higher-order evidence and claim-preservation hierarchy.

1. **Keep one central contribution visible.**
2. **Require an evidential warrant for every contestable proposition.**
3. **Make each substantive paragraph carry a warranted proposition or necessary procedural function.**
4. **Use triangular organisation and control what the reader can reasonably know at each point.**
5. **Keep the manuscript inside its discipline.**
6. **Present the research argument rather than the project travelogue.**
7. **Do not use the target journal's name rhetorically as a proxy for fit or quality.**
8. **Make sections and sentences do visible work; prefer direct, concrete prose.**
9. **Use literature to position the argument rather than substitute for it.**
10. **Expose only as much method or theory as the contribution needs.**
11. **Match claim strength to evidential strength.**
12. **Use robustness to answer a named threat rather than display analytical activity.**
13. **Treat limitations as justified scope boundaries rather than generic weakness lists.**

## Why Rules 2, 3, 11 and 12 matter especially in AI-assisted work

Automated or AI-assisted updating can produce new text and output faster than a researcher can inspect the inferential language built around it.

Those four rules force the writing layer to ask:

- what licenses the proposition;
- whether the paragraph does substantive work;
- whether the wording has outrun the evidence;
- whether robustness addresses a real threat rather than signalling diligence.
