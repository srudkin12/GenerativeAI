# Academic Writing Filter: a social-science overview

The **Academic Writing Filter (AWF)** is an operational manuscript-governance artefact.

It should not be understood as a style checker or a set of thirteen equivalent writing preferences.

## The governing hierarchy

The October 2026 version gives decision rights in the following order:

1. **verified evidence and mathematical/statistical correctness**;
2. **project-specific claim-preservation contract**;
3. **binding outlet/editor/referee requirements compatible with 1–2**;
4. **outlet-specific corpus or style overlay**;
5. **cross-project writing heuristics and default revision rules**;
6. **local house-style preferences**.

Lower-priority requirements may shape the manuscript only where they do not conflict with higher-priority constraints.

## Why this matters with generative AI

Language models are good at producing fluent alternatives.

Fluency creates a research risk when a plausible rewrite:

- drops a caveat;
- turns a descriptive result into an inferential one;
- turns an inferential result into a causal one;
- treats robustness activity as evidence of universal validity;
- changes the disciplinary object of the paper;
- retains a polished paragraph after its supporting result has changed.

The filter therefore asks a harder question than "is this well written?":

> **What exact evidence or reasoning licenses this wording, and does the revision preserve the authorised claim?**

## Four warrant types

The framework recognises different sources of warrant:

- academic literature;
- manuscript-generated evidence;
- authoritative primary evidence;
- transparent analytic reasoning.

The appropriate warrant depends on the proposition.

A citation is not a generic substitute for evidence.

## Use with quantitative and qualitative research

The architecture is not restricted to statistical models.

A manuscript-generated research object may be:

- an estimate or model output;
- a qualitative theme supported by a documented analytic procedure;
- an archival finding;
- an administrative series;
- a simulation;
- a formal derivation;
- a coded text corpus;
- another project-specific verified object.

## Use does not imply AI

The Academic Writing Filter can govern human-only, collaborative or AI-assisted revision.

Its relevance to this repository arises because generative AI can produce substantial manuscript revisions quickly, increasing the value of an explicit claim boundary.

## Provenance and public artefact

John H. Cochrane's *Writing Tips for Ph.D. Students* is the principal architectural starting point for part of the operational writing guidance. The AWF is an independent implementation and should not imply that Cochrane authored, approved or endorsed it.

The filter also draws on broader research-integrity principles and practice-derived governance rules.
