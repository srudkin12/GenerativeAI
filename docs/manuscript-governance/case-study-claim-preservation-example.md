# Worked claim-preservation example

A claim-preservation contract is most useful when a result could be rewritten in several plausible but substantively different ways.

The examples below come from the documented educational-data case used elsewhere in this resource. The application is secondary; the purpose is to show what a claim boundary looks like in practice.

## Example A — a complex method versus a simpler comparator

| Contract field | Worked example |
|---|---|
| **Verified evidence** | The first principal component explains approximately 97.31% of variation in the three published domain means. |
| **Authorised claim** | PCA provides a compact global summary of the dominant structure; Ball Mapper can complement it when local overlapping neighbourhood and profile relationships are substantively relevant. |
| **Prohibited claim** | Ball Mapper reveals a fundamentally different global structure that conventional summaries fail to detect. |
| **Inferential boundary** | Dominant global dimensionality and local overlapping neighbourhood structure answer different questions. |
| **Required qualification** | Ball Mapper is a complement in this application, not a replacement for simpler global summaries. |
| **Frozen distinction** | Algorithmic robustness is not the same as uncertainty arising from the underlying survey and measurement process. |

### Why the contract matters

A later rewrite could make the method sound more novel by removing "complement", "local" or "in this application".

That revision might improve rhetorical force while making the claim less faithful to the evidence.

The claim-preservation contract makes those words substantive rather than stylistic.

## Example B — a computed quantity with an unidentified interpretation

| Contract field | Worked example |
|---|---|
| **Observed/generated quantity** | A numerical summary based on overlapping endpoint memberships could be computed. |
| **Prohibited claim** | The quantity identifies direct movement of the same observational units between two specific neighbourhoods over time. |
| **Inferential boundary** | Overlapping endpoint memberships do not identify a same-unit coupling between the two neighbourhoods. |
| **Replacement evidence** | Observable endpoint states and robustness across alternative constructions. |
| **Authorised claim** | The analysis may describe endpoint-state movement and the robustness of the broad directional pattern without claiming an unidentified direct flow. |
| **Frozen distinction** | Change in neighbourhood occupancy is not direct same-unit movement. |

## Historical caution

The table shows how a claim-preservation contract can encode the **current authorised argument**.

It should not be read as evidence that a formal contract already existed at the moment the original empirical corrections were made.

The contract is applied retrospectively to the mature claim boundary for illustration.
