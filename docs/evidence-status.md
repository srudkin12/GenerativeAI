# Evidence-status rules

The resource distinguishes four evidence classes.

| Status | Meaning | Appropriate public use |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified verbatim record** | Wording checked against an original export, screenshot or authoritative transcript | May be quoted with a source note |
| **Conversation-history reconstruction** | A recovered summary or wording that has not been checked against the original record | Paraphrase; do not present as a quotation |
| **Frozen project evidence** | Manuscript, output, figure, log, handover or other version-controlled project artefact | May support claims within its actual evidential scope |
| **Current interpretation** | A later claim about what an episode demonstrates | Label as interpretation and keep it narrower than the underlying evidence |

## Why the distinction matters

A clean narrative written after the event can make contribution appear more certain than it was.

A version history can show that a sentence changed without establishing why it changed. A transcript can show that someone claimed to have performed a check without proving that the check was executed correctly. A local execution log can show that code ran without proving that the research design was scientifically appropriate.

Evidence should therefore be matched to the claim.

## Public quotation rule

Do not convert reconstructed wording into a quotation merely because a quotation would be more vivid.

Before publication, every quotation from an AI conversation should be checked against the original export, screenshot or equivalent authoritative record.
