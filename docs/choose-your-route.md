# Find the right response to a research problem

Begin with the problem that is holding up the research. The [field guide](field-guide.html) gives a compact starting protocol; the [ten-step protocol](practical-research-protocol.html) explains the wider sequence. Neither asks researchers to complete every template.

**Before sharing source material:** check whether institutional and ethics requirements, consent, third-party rights, confidentiality agreements and service-specific terms permit disclosure to the proposed AI system. If permission is uncertain, do not transfer the material. An authorised environment or wholly synthetic example may be an alternative. **Summaries and paraphrases can also disclose restricted, identifying or sensitive information**; reassess the content against the same rules. Removing names alone does not guarantee safe disclosure.

## What constitutes a useful next step?

A generated result that was never checked requires evidence production, not more polished writing. A checked result with an over-extended claim requires an inferential revision. A defensible claim that has become unclear can be edited under its existing boundaries. A claim about who supplied an idea requires a distinct trace, which is best preserved **when the decision occurs**, even though attribution is assessed later.

For a compact stage-to-record reference, see the [protocol crosswalk](protocol-crosswalk.html). For a single invented decision followed through the stages, use the [constructed illustration](illustrated-workflow.html).
