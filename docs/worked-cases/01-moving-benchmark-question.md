# Case 1 — When AI proposes a consequential research question

## Research setting

The project began with a public PISA release and an existing researcher interest in ranking systems and multidimensional representations of educational performance.

Generative AI entered after that starting point.

## Consequential move

During the initial exploration, the earliest recoverable explicit formulation of the **moving-benchmark question** appeared in AI output: could apparent relative improvement occur because outcomes elsewhere deteriorated more rapidly rather than because the focal system improved?

The researcher accepted that question and moved it into the empirical programme.

## What happened next

The final empirical design did not preserve a simple rhetorical "did Britain improve?" framing. It distinguished:

- change in a system's own published performance;
- change in relative rank;
- movement within a changing multidomain international comparison field.

## Reason-provenance lesson

**Origination can be traceably AI-originated without making the subsequent scholarship AI-owned.**

Question proposal and complete research authorship are different claims. Selection, specification, implementation, checking and interpretation remained consequential later in the workflow.

## Evidence status

The proposal origin is supported by recovered conversation evidence and frozen downstream manuscript development.

**Public-release control:** insert a direct quotation only after the wording has been checked against the original ChatGPT export or screenshot.

## Boundary

The record cannot prove that no similar idea existed privately in the researcher's mind before the AI phrased it.

The defensible claim is narrower: within the traceable recorded workflow, the earliest recoverable explicit formulation was AI-originated.
