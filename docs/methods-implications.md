# Methods and implications

The resource treats generative AI as a participant in a **sequence of research functions**, not as a single intervention labelled "AI use".

That distinction has several implications for social-science research practice.

## 1. AI contribution is function-specific

A model may originate a useful question, draft code, propose a comparison, criticise an interpretation, reorganise a manuscript or produce a misleading inference.

Those are different contributions with different evidential consequences.

The relevant question is not simply:

> Did AI contribute?

A more useful question is:

> **What function did AI perform, what authority did that function carry, and what happened before its output influenced the research claim?**

## 2. Proposal generation and evidence production are different

A generated reference, code block, theme, interpretation or analytical suggestion is a **candidate research artefact**.

Generation does not verify it.

Verification depends on the design:

- local execution and diagnostics for computational analysis;
- source inspection for documentary or archival work;
- documented coding and interpretive procedures for qualitative analysis;
- appropriate validation, triangulation or replication elsewhere.

The boundary protects research from treating fluency or executability as evidence.

## 3. Researcher contribution includes constraint and refusal

Human contribution is often described too narrowly as origination.

Substantive contribution may instead appear through:

- defining the unit of analysis;
- rejecting inappropriate aggregation;
- preserving known caveats;
- selecting the inferential target;
- refusing an attractive but unsupported interpretation;
- deciding what evidence would be sufficient.

A research workflow should therefore record consequential **specification decisions**, not only novel ideas.

## 4. Robustness should be allowed to make the paper less exciting

A useful comparator or robustness check answers a named threat.

If the result shows that a simpler explanation captures most of the structure, the preferred methodological claim should become smaller.

Robustness is therefore not a display of analytical activity. It is a mechanism for revising the claim.

## 5. Correct computation does not guarantee valid inference

A numerical quantity can be computed correctly while the interpretation attached to it requires an unidentified relationship, missing comparison or stronger design.

AI-assisted research particularly benefits from separating:

**what was computed**  
from  
**what the researcher is entitled to infer from it**.

## 6. Manuscript revision is part of research governance

Writing is not merely presentation after the research is complete.

A rewrite can:

- remove a qualification;
- strengthen a descriptive claim into an inferential one;
- turn association into causation;
- collapse a conceptual distinction;
- make a complementary method sound like a replacement.

The claim therefore needs governance through the writing stage.

## 7. Provenance should be claim-relative, not exhaustive

Reason provenance does not require recording every prompt or action.

The useful record is the one needed to support later claims about consequential contribution.

Relevant evidence may include:

- ex-ante principles;
- contemporaneous decision records;
- versioned analytical artefacts;
- frozen evidence;
- later reconstruction clearly labelled as retrospective.

More records are not automatically better if they do not discriminate between the contribution histories that matter.

## 8. Attribution and desirability are separate

A workflow can be transparently attributable without being a division of labour that a researcher, journal, university or discipline should endorse.

Reason provenance can clarify what happened.

It does not by itself decide what **should** happen.

## 9. The method is application-independent

The architecture is intended to travel across:

- quantitative social science;
- qualitative analysis;
- computational research;
- archival and documentary research;
- mixed-method designs.

Transfer requires design-appropriate evidence standards, inferential boundaries and disciplinary judgement.

---

# Practical implication

The overall change is simple:

**Treat generative AI as a source of proposals and transformations inside a governed research process, not as an authority whose output carries the same status at every stage.**
