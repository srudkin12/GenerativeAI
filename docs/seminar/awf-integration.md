# Integrating manuscript governance into the seminar

The Academic Writing Filter should enter the seminar as part of the research process, not as a separate presentation about writing advice.

## Recommended location

Insert the manuscript-governance material **after the empirical claim has been narrowed/corrected and before the seminar moves fully into retrospective reason-provenance theory**.

The audience has then seen:

- useful AI origination;
- researcher specification;
- generated analysis and local verification;
- inconvenient comparator evidence;
- an unwarranted inference;
- correction to a defensible empirical claim.

The natural next question is:

> **What happens when we rewrite the paper after deciding what the evidence actually supports?**

## Main slide 1 — Writing creates another failure point

### Suggested visual

**verified evidence**  
→ **authorised claim**  
→ **manuscript revision**  
→ **published claim**

Place a warning marker between authorised claim and manuscript revision:

**fluent rewriting can alter inferential force**

### Spoken point

Writing is not merely the cosmetic stage after analysis. Removing a qualifier, changing "associated with" to "caused", or describing a complement as a replacement can change the research argument without any new evidence.

## Main slide 2 — Academic Writing Filter as manuscript governance

Use three stacked blocks:

### TASK-RELATIVE INVARIANTS
evidence · inferential boundaries · qualifications · frozen distinctions

↓ constrain

### CONTEXTUAL CONSTRAINTS
discipline · manuscript type · outlet/editor requirements

↓ constrain

### MUTABLE PRESENTATION
wording · order · transitions · signposting

Short footer:

**Generative AI may transform presentation; it must not silently transform the authorised claim.**

### Spoken point

The filter is not "my preferred style". It allocates decision rights. Evidence and authorised claim boundaries outrank outlet style and house preferences.

## Backup / optional main slide — worked claim-preservation example

Use the PCA/Ball Mapper example:

- **verified:** PC1 ≈ 97.31%;
- **authorised:** PCA is the compact global summary; BM complements it for local overlap/profile relations;
- **prohibited:** BM reveals a fundamentally different hidden global structure;
- **qualification:** complement, not replacement.

A second line can mention B4→B9:

- direct same-system flow claim is prohibited after the coupling problem was identified.

## Transition into reason provenance

A useful transition is:

**Manuscript governance protects the claim once we know what the evidence authorises. But how do we justify our later story about who or what contributed to getting us there?**

That moves directly into retrospective storytelling and reason provenance.

## Timing

Aim for roughly **3–4 minutes** across the two main slides.

Keep the detailed worked contract as backup unless rehearsal shows that the audience needs the concrete example in the main sequence.

## Claims not to make

Do not say that:

- the Academic Writing Filter caused the original empirical corrections;
- the worked empirical project validates the filter;
- the filter has been shown to improve publication outcomes or writing quality;
- Cochrane authored or endorsed the filter;
- use of the filter necessarily implies use of AI.
